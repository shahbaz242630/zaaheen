AI readiness assessment in the UAE: what to expect

An AI readiness assessment looks at how your teams really work and tells you, team by team, where AI will save time and money and where it will not. A good one is done on site, ends in a written report with costs and payback, and comes from someone who sells no tools.

By Zaaheen · Last updated 26 November 2026

What does an AI readiness assessment cover?

It covers the work itself: who does what, how long each step takes, where things wait, and which steps a machine could do better than a person. The output is a decision for each team, not a score out of ten.

Zaaheen is an independent AI consultancy based in Dubai that assesses businesses across the UAE, Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Kuwait, Bahrain and Oman on site. We sell and build no tools, and our team spent twenty years in corporate operations before doing this work.

Here is what our AI readiness assessment in Dubai and across the Gulf covers, step by step:

  1. A free 30-minute call. You tell us about your business and what is not working. We tell you honestly whether an assessment is worth it for you.
  2. We walk every role, on site. From reception to the managing director, we sit with the people doing the work, watch how it really gets done and time each step.
  3. We map and score it. Every process becomes a map with timed steps, hand-offs and waiting points. Each step is scored on volume, repetition, rules, data and the cost of a mistake.
  4. Report and read-out. A written report and a meeting with your team: where AI helps, where it does not, what to do first, what it costs and when it pays back.

Each team gets one of four verdicts, with the reasons behind it:

  • AI now: work that reads, writes, sorts or answers, at volume, where the data already exists.
  • Automate without AI: fixed rules and clean inputs, where a form, a workflow or a link between two systems does it cheaper and safer. Our guide to AI vs automation explains the difference.
  • Fix the process first: too many exceptions, unclear ownership or missing data. AI on a broken process only makes it break faster.
  • Leave as it is: low volume, or expert judgement where a mistake is costly. Some work should stay with your people.

For every recommendation the report gives the effort, a cost range, the hours it saves each month, when it pays back, and where a person must stay in charge. If you would rather look for candidates yourself first, our guide on where AI helps your business shows the method.

How ready are Gulf businesses for AI?

Most GCC organisations plan to use AI, but far fewer have the data, the rules or the way of working to make it pay. That gap is what an assessment is for.

In its February 2026 report, Roland Berger found that nearly 80% of GCC organisations have AI in their strategic plans, but only one-third have an enterprise-wide data strategy and fewer than one in three have the operating model to scale it. Its first recommended action was organisation-wide AI readiness assessments.

The same pattern shows up in Deloitte's June 2026 survey of more than 3,200 leaders in 24 countries: 84% of organisations had not yet redesigned jobs or workflows around AI (Deloitte Middle East, 4 June 2026). Boston Consulting Group put it more bluntly in October 2024: 74% of companies had yet to show tangible value from AI, and leaders put about 70% of their effort into people and processes, not technology (BCG, 1,000 executives in 59 countries).

Whether you run a 40-person firm in Abu Dhabi or a large company in Riyadh, the lesson is the same: the problem is rarely the technology. It is the way the work is done. Our article on why AI projects fail goes through the named studies.

Is a free online quiz or "free AI audit" enough?

A free quiz is a fair starting point, but it scores your own answers, and a free audit from a company that builds AI is usually the first step towards selling you its own work. Neither sees how your people really work.

Many searches in Dubai and the UAE are for an "AI audit for my business". The word is understandable, but an AI readiness assessment is not a financial audit, an assurance report or a certification of any AI system. It is an operational review of where AI can help.

How do you check an AI consultant is independent?

Ask three plain questions, and get the answers in writing:

  • Do you sell, resell or build any AI product, software or hardware?
  • Do you take commissions, referral fees or kickbacks from any provider?
  • Are you a partner or reseller of any provider?

If the answer to any of these is yes, their advice may still be good, but it is not independent. An adviser who earns from a tool has a reason to recommend it.

Our answer to all three is no. Our only income is the fee you pay us. If we name a product, it is because it fits your work, and you buy it directly from its maker. What about data protection in the UAE and Saudi Arabia?

Your adviser will see how your business runs, so data protection should be agreed before the first visit, not after. This section is general information, not legal advice.

In the UAE, personal data is covered by the Personal Data Protection Law, Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021, alongside the separate rules of free zones such as DIFC and ADGM. Saudi Arabia has its own Personal Data Protection Law, and Qatar, Bahrain, Kuwait and Oman each have their own rules too. Take advice from a lawyer on what applies to your business.

What you can ask of any adviser is simple:

  • A confidentiality agreement signed before the first visit.
  • Roles and processes recorded, not people's names or personal details.
  • A clear date when notes are deleted.

That is how we work: we sign the agreement first, record roles rather than people, and delete our notes 90 days after the report unless you ask us in writing to keep them for a follow-on project.

For a small firm, it usually takes two to three weeks from the first visit to the read-out. Larger businesses with more teams and sites take longer, and we agree the timeline in writing before we start.

After the free call, once we know how many teams and sites are involved, we send a written quote: one fixed price in UAE dirhams, plus VAT where it applies, set out in full, showing what each stage covers. There are no hidden costs, and nothing is added later without your written agreement.
