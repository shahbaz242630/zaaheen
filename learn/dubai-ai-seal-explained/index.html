The Dubai AI Seal explained: what it is and who it is for

The Dubai AI Seal is a free classification from the Dubai Centre for Artificial Intelligence for companies in Dubai that provide AI products or services. It ranks them in six tiers, from E up to S, and Dubai government buyers look for it when they hire AI suppliers. It is not a certificate for businesses that only use AI, but if you buy AI it tells you which suppliers have been checked.

By Zaaheen · Last updated 26 November 2026

Zaaheen is an independent AI consultancy based in Dubai that assesses businesses across the Gulf, on site and role by role. We are not a holder, issuer or partner of the Dubai AI Seal or of any scheme on this page. The official site, dub.ai/en/ai-seal, is the authority on its rules; we last checked it on 26 November 2026.

What is the Dubai AI Seal?

The Dubai AI Seal is a verification system run by the Dubai Centre for Artificial Intelligence (DCAI), in an initiative overseen by the Dubai Future Foundation. It was launched in January 2025 under the directives of Sheikh Hamdan bin Mohammed, Crown Prince of Dubai, as part of the Dubai Universal Blueprint for AI (Dubai Media Office, 22 April 2025).

Its job is to separate genuine AI providers from companies that only use the word. DCAI says the seal goes only to businesses that "genuinely provide AI technology", and lists "protection from irrelevant suppliers and AI washing" as a benefit for the government and private companies that buy from them (DCAI). Each certified company gets a seal showing its tier and a unique serial number, which anyone can check on the seal's website.

The first holders were presented at Dubai AI Week in April 2025. By 15 May 2025, 325 companies operating in Dubai had applied, representing 77 international offices, and e& and IBM were among the first awarded the top tier (Dubai Media Office, 15 May 2025). We found no published count of how many companies hold the seal today.

Who can apply for the Dubai AI Seal?

Companies that provide AI, not companies that use it. The scheme is open to local, regional and international companies licensed to operate in Dubai that offer AI products or services, or build AI into what they sell (Dubai Media Office). DCAI says AI businesses of any size can apply.

The National reported five categories of activity: AI consulting, development, infrastructure, integration and ancillary services (The National, 19 August 2025). A restaurant group using an AI chatbot, or a logistics firm using AI to plan routes, is a user of AI and is not who the seal is for. If your business sells a product or service with AI inside it, you may be eligible, and DCAI decides.

What do the Dubai AI Seal tiers mean?

There are six tiers: E, D, C, B, A and S, with S marking the highest impact on Dubai's AI economy. DCAI places each company using its Dubai AI Business Activity Classification System, based on the nature of its activities and services, its current and future projects, and its economic contribution to the AI sector (DCAI).

The assessment looks at the nature of the company's AI work, the number of specialised AI engineers it employs, its current and upcoming projects, and its partnerships with government and the private sector (Dubai Media Office). DCAI has not published what each tier requires, how long a seal lasts or how renewal works. Any page that gives exact tier thresholds is guessing; ask DCAI.

How do you get the Dubai AI Seal?

You apply online, and it costs nothing. DCAI describes three steps (DCAI):

  1. Apply. Submit the online form at dub.ai/en/ai-seal with your business information.
  2. Classification. The DCAI team reviews the application and places the company in a tier.
  3. Seal issued. Approved companies receive a personalised seal with their tier and serial number, and may show it on their websites and marketing.

Be careful with anyone offering to "get you the seal" for a fee. The application is free and goes straight to DCAI.

Do you need the seal for Dubai government AI contracts?

In practice, yes. Dubai's Media Office said in April 2025 that any technology company wishing to take part in government AI projects in Dubai "must first obtain" the seal (Dubai Media Office). On 19 October 2025, Dubai Finance urged government entities to restrict AI-related service and consultancy contracts to companies holding it, and to tell consultancies and AI providers that they need it (Dubai Media Office).

That rule is about Dubai government buyers. We found no published requirement that private companies, other emirates or federal bodies use it, so for a private business it is a useful check, not an obligation.

What does the seal mean if your business is buying AI?

It answers one question well: is this supplier a real AI company, active in Dubai? It does not answer the question that decides whether your project works: does this supplier's product fit the way your people actually work?

That second question is where most Gulf businesses are thin. Roland Berger's Middle East report of 10 February 2026 found most GCC organisations plan to use AI, while far fewer have the data strategy or the governance to run it:

A seal on a supplier's website cannot close that gap for you. Before you compare sealed suppliers, it pays to know four things about your own business:

  • Where AI would actually help. Which tasks read, write, sort or answer at volume, and which are better served by a simple rule or form. Our guide to AI vs automation explains the difference.
  • How the work is done today. A mapped process, with its hand-offs and waiting points, is the brief a supplier needs. Without it, you are buying from a demo.
  • Where a person stays in charge. Which decisions are too costly to get wrong to hand to a system, and who checks the output.
  • What data the tool would touch. Which records it reads, where they live, and who in your team owns them.

With those answers, the seal becomes a useful second filter: you shortlist suppliers that fit your work, then check their serial numbers on the DCAI site. Projects that skip the first step are the ones that stall, as our guide to why AI projects fail sets out.

Are there similar AI certification schemes elsewhere in the Gulf?

A few, and they work differently. In Saudi Arabia, the Saudi Data and AI Authority (SDAIA) awards AI ethics badges to organisations based on their maturity in applying its AI Ethics Principles. Press reports describe five levels and one-year validity, and report that more than 20 companies received badges at the UNESCO Global Forum on the Ethics of AI in Riyadh in 2026. The level names differ between reports, so check SDAIA directly.

At federal level in the UAE, the Cyber Security Council announced a national AI test and validation lab in May 2026 to assess AI models, agents and applications, with a national certification mark for systems that pass. That certifies AI systems, not the businesses using them. We found no comparable company scheme in Abu Dhabi or Qatar at the time of writing.

Common questions about the Dubai AI Seal

Is the Dubai AI Seal free?

Yes. DCAI says the service is free of charge, and you apply directly on its website at dub.ai/en/ai-seal.

Can a company that only uses AI get the Dubai AI Seal?

Not on the published rules. The seal is for companies licensed in Dubai that provide AI products or services, or build AI into what they offer. A business that uses AI tools internally is a buyer of AI, not a provider.

How do I check if a supplier has the Dubai AI Seal?

Ask for its serial number and check it on the Dubai AI Seal website, which DCAI says can verify any seal. A logo on a website alone proves nothing.

How long does the Dubai AI Seal last?

DCAI has not published a validity period or renewal process. Ask DCAI directly before relying on an old seal.

Is the Dubai AI Seal the same as a free AI certification from the Dubai government?

No. The seal is a classification for companies. Free AI courses and certificates for individuals are separate programmes, and completing one does not give a company the seal.

Does Zaaheen hold the Dubai AI Seal?

No. We are an independent consultancy and have no role in the scheme. We assess private businesses that want to use AI, and the seal's own site is the authority on its rules.
